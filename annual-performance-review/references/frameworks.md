# Review frameworks at top tech companies

Use this to match a company's format when the user names it, or to borrow good structure. Company processes change every few years: if the user pastes their actual form, ladder, or rating scale, it always wins over this file. Don't claim the person's company uses one of these unless the user said so.

## Google: GRAD (Googler Reviews and Development)

- Annual rating, with regular check-ins on development during the year.
- Rating scale is about **impact**: Not Enough Impact → Moderate Impact → Significant Impact (most people) → Outstanding Impact → Transformative Impact.
- What's judged: the impact of results against role and level expectations, plus how the person works (collaboration, "Googleyness", leadership).
- Writing style that works: the **XYZ formula**, "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]". Lead with impact, quantify, show scope.

## Meta: PSC (Performance Summary Cycle)

- Engineering is assessed on four axes: **Project Impact**, **Engineering Excellence**, **Direction**, **People**. Expectations on each axis grow with level (E3 → E7+).
- The rating scale runs from below expectations up to "Redefines" (the exact labels have changed between cycles). Ratings are calibrated across teams.
- Self-review, peer reviews, and manager review all feed in. Peer reviews are short and specific: what the person did, what impact it had, what they could do better.
- Useful idea: write evidence per axis, and make sure Senior+ people have real Direction and People evidence, not just Project Impact.

## Amazon: Leadership Principles, Forte, OLR

- **Forte**: the employee and peers describe strengths ("superpowers") and growth areas; it's narrative, not ratings.
- Evaluation uses the **Leadership Principles** (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Invent and Simplify, Dive Deep, Deliver Results, Insist on the Highest Standards, Earn Trust, and the others). Reviews and promo docs cite LPs with concrete examples.
- **OLR** (Organization Level Review) is where managers calibrate ratings and promotions. Promo documents are written narratives with evidence of sustained next-level work.
- Useful idea: tag each achievement with 1–2 principles or values, if the company has published values.

## Microsoft: Connects

- Regular check-ins rather than a single annual form. Impact is framed in three circles:
  1. **Individual accomplishments** that contribute to team/business results.
  2. **Contributions to others' success**.
  3. **Results that build on the work, ideas, or effort of others**.
- Useful idea: the second and third circles are a good checklist for collaboration evidence at any company.

## Netflix

- Historically no formal ratings. Instead written **360 feedback**, often in a **Start / Stop / Continue** shape, signed (not anonymous).
- The "keeper test" is a manager heuristic: would I fight to keep this person?
- Useful idea: Start/Stop/Continue is a good default shape for **peer** mode.

## GitLab (public handbook)

- Values **CREDIT**: Collaboration, Results, Efficiency, Diversity/Inclusion/Belonging, Iteration, Transparency.
- Talent assessment crosses **performance** (Developing / Performing / Exceeding) with **growth potential**, a 9-box style grid.
- Useful idea: separate "how they performed this year" from "how fast they're growing". They're different questions.

## Career ladders (engineering)

Many companies publish ladders: see progression.fyi for a collection (Dropbox, Rent the Runway, CircleCI, Medium, and others). A common progression:

| Level | Scope | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Junior | Well-defined tasks | Learns the codebase, delivers with guidance |
| Middle | Features | Delivers independently, owns quality of their work |
| Senior | Projects / a system | Leads design, unblocks others, owns production, mentors |
| Staff | Multiple teams / domain | Sets technical direction, solves ambiguous cross-team problems |
| Principal | Org / company | Shapes strategy and long-term architecture |

Staff+ archetypes (Will Larson, *Staff Engineer*): Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand. A promo case for Staff should show which archetype the person operates as.

For engineering managers, typical axes: delivery, people development, hiring, team health, cross-team influence, technical judgment.

## Writing techniques

- **XYZ** (Google): accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. For achievements.
- **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Longer form of the same idea.
- **SBI** (Center for Creative Leadership): Situation, Behavior, Impact. For feedback and growth areas.
- **Brag document** (Julia Evans): a running list of the year's work with links and impact, kept by the employee. The best input for any review.
- **Radical Candor** (Kim Scott): care personally, challenge directly. The tone for growth areas.

## Common rating scales (for mapping)

| Generic | Google GRAD | Meta-style | Typical 5-point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Below | Not Enough Impact | Below / Meets Some | 1–2 |
| Meets | Moderate / Significant Impact | Meets Most / Meets All | 3 |
| Exceeds | Outstanding Impact | Exceeds / Greatly Exceeds | 4 |
| Far exceeds | Transformative Impact | Redefines | 5 |

This mapping is approximate; use it only to translate wording, never to assign a rating on the company's behalf.
